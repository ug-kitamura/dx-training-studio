# Tackling the advanced task (day 2)

## Learning goals

This is day 2 of the hands-on, and the final quest of this training. In today's 60 minutes you'll finish your Part 2 implementation, take it through review with your partner and merge it, and **present your own code in your own words for two minutes**. When that's done, you'll have completed every series of the DX training.

Let's settle one thing up front. **Today works even if you haven't fully solved Part 2.** The talks aren't a showcase for people who finished; they're where everyone brings their own ideas and their own sticking points.

## Today's schedule

```text
00-05  Progress check (the organizers just ask for a show of hands. Saying you're stuck is fine)
05-30  Part 2 implementation (25 minutes)
30-38  Create a PR → partner reviews → approves → merge
38-54  Talks: 2 minutes each × 8 people
54-60  Wrap-up
```

## Finishing your implementation

You have 25 minutes to write as far as you can get. Match your branch to the state you're in.

- If you've **already been working on a side route (branch)** over the days in between, carry on from there
- If you **committed straight to main**, you can branch off from main as it stands and carry on (in this case you can't produce a diff to put in a PR, so flag it with the organizers)
- If you're **starting today**, branch off onto a side route first, the same as day 1

- If you finished over the days in between, go over the behavior and polish it. Look through the four weeks of output and check by eye that no pair from the previous week lands in the same group — that gives you something concrete to show in your talk
- If you're partway, finish **by stopping at the last point where it works**. Even if it isn't all there — "the four weeks of output run," say — that's today's result
- If you're stuck, put **where you got stuck and what you tried** into words. That becomes your material for the talk directly, and it shows everyone else a pitfall they haven't hit yet

If you get into trouble, do the same as on day 1. Ask the internal AI chat, talk to your partner, flag it with the organizers — use any of it.

## PR → review → merge

The flow is exactly the same as day 1 (look back at the table on the day 1 page for the steps). There are only two differences.

- **You may open a PR even if you're partway.** You merge as far as you got. What matters is that "the result as of today" is left on main
- Point your review questions at **the parts of Part 2 where you had to be inventive**. "How did you satisfy this condition?" — you'll find questions come to mind more naturally than on day 1. Practicing your answers is a warm-up for the talk right afterwards

If you have no diff to submit, spend this time **reviewing your partner's PR**. Asking questions doesn't require having opened a PR yourself. If you did merge, go back to main and run it, the same as day 1 — that's the screen you'll show in your talk.

## Giving your talk — three points in two minutes

Everyone speaks for two minutes. The organizers announce the order and how to show your screen on the day. There are only three things to say.

| Order | What to say | Approx. time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Show the part that works** (run it and show the JSON output) | 30 seconds |
| 2 | **One thing you're proud of** (how you split it, why you wrote it that way) | 1 minute |
| 3 | **One thing you got stuck on** (where, and what you tried) | 30 seconds |

If you're partway, the shape changes a little.

| Order | What to say | Approx. time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Show how far you got (running just the part that works is fine) | 30 seconds |
| 2 | Where you got stuck and what you tried | 1 minute |
| 3 | What you're currently thinking of trying next | 30 seconds |

If Part 2 doesn't run, **showing the Part 1 you finished on day 1 is plenty**. That's your tool today too.

Either way, the same thing holds: **explain the code you wrote in your own words**. Even if you wrote it while asking an AI, you're the one who chose, assembled, and ran it. There's no need to speak well — **sharing where you got stuck is itself a lesson for the other participants**.

## There's more than one way to solve it

Listening to eight talks, you'll notice that **the solutions to the same task are all over the place**. That's the greatest value in these talks.

A mathematically constructed solution and a plainly assembled one are both correct if they satisfy the conditions and run. If a way of splitting makes you think "I never thought of that," take it home without hesitation. Borrowing from your colleagues' code is a perfectly proper way to learn in the world of shared vaults.
